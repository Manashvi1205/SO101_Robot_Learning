# ACT target highlighting and appearance generalization

Date: 2026-11-15

## Scope and feasibility

The proposed experiment is feasible without changing the 40 demonstrations or
their action labels.  It is not yet evidence that annotation causes object
generalization.  Highlighting can canonicalize a selected object, while
appearance augmentation can reduce dependence on the FedEx carton's black/white
texture, but both depend on accurate visible-object masks.  At deployment this
moves part of the problem into live target detection.  Policy and detector
generalization therefore need separate measurements.

The source dataset and 50k checkpoint passed the audit recorded in
`outputs/target_augmentation_prototype/audit.json`.  The checkpoint is a usable
inference/warm-start package, not a resumable optimizer checkpoint: its 050000
directory contains only `pretrained_model/`, with no optimizer, scheduler,
random-state, or training-step state.

## What is in the demonstrations

- 40 episodes, 25,062 frames, 30 fps; episode lengths are 508–786 frames.
- Two synchronized 1280x720 H.264 views named exactly
  `observation.images.wrist` and `observation.images.phone`.
- Six calibrated position observations and six position-command actions in
  shoulder-pan, shoulder-lift, elbow-flex, wrist-flex, wrist-roll, gripper order.
- One instruction: pick up the box and place it in the fixed target area.
- The manipulated object is a branded, open-top, black/white carton.  Start pose
  and visible faces vary.  The destination is a white paper sheet with a blue
  square.  The operator, gripper, destination paper, and carton all create
  possible visual confounds.

All vectors and timestamps are finite.  Frame indices, episode ranges, and
1/30-second timestamps are contiguous.  Commands are not copies of measured
state: the minimum mean squared action/state discrepancy occurs when measured
state is shifted about four frames after action (approximately 133 ms).  This is
consistent with command/actuation lag.  It must be retained identically in all
derived variants; it should not be "fixed" for only one arm of the experiment.

## Annotation plan

Use visible-object masks, not bounding boxes, because boxes would recolor the
mat, paper, gripper, and background.  A practical offline workflow is:

1. Manually segment the carton on the first clear frame of every episode and at
   phase changes (pre-grasp, grasp, transport, placement, release).
2. Propagate each mask bidirectionally with a video object segmenter/tracker
   such as SAM 2 video propagation.  This is a proposed tool, not an audited
   dependency in the current environment.
3. Force new manual keyframes after full occlusion, abrupt wrist motion, or a
   view-boundary crossing.  Do not interpolate through a fully invisible box.
4. Review both views frame-by-frame at phase boundaries and sample at least 10%
   of intermediate frames.  Record visibility (`visible`, `partial`,
   `fully_occluded`) and a review status.  Empty masks are correct when fully
   occluded.
5. Reject tracks that leak onto the gripper, destination paper, operator, or
   shadows.  Split masks at true occlusion boundaries rather than painting over
   the occluder.

The two cameras should share object identity and episode phase but retain their
own mask.  Their geometric projections are different, so one view's mask must
never be copied to the other.

Likely label inconsistencies are partial/open carton faces, severe motion blur,
the carton leaving the wrist field of view, gripper overlap, and the carton
overlapping the destination paper after placement.  A tracker may also attach
to the stable blue destination instead of the moving carton.  These cases need
explicit review flags; model confidence alone is not ground truth.

## Canonical target representation

The prototype uses a 30% fixed cyan interior tint with a thick white outer and
thin black inner contour.  It is independent of the carton's appearance,
survives image downsampling better than a one-pixel outline, and stays visible
on light and dark backgrounds.  The representation is generated by
`target_augmentation.highlight_target`.

Exactly the same renderer can operate on a live BGR camera frame once an online
mask is available.  That does not solve live selection: for the one-object
workspace, begin with a workspace-restricted generic object mask and temporal
tracking, then validate it against held-out colored boxes.  Evaluate two modes:

- **oracle-mask policy test:** reviewed/manual masks isolate whether ACT benefits
  from canonicalization;
- **online-mask system test:** the actual detector/tracker measures end-to-end
  performance and mask failure rate.

Without this split, a detector failure could be mistaken for a policy failure,
or oracle annotation could overstate deployable performance.

## Appearance augmentation

The prototype replaces Lab chroma inside the visible mask while retaining the
original lightness and low-frequency texture.  This preserves the silhouette,
pose, folds, shading, and all recorded state/action labels.  Production variants
should sample an episode-level material identity from colors and restrained
cardboard/plastic textures, then keep it temporally stable in both cameras.

Rules for label-preserving generation:

- never warp, translate, scale, rotate, or paste the object;
- never alter the gripper, destination, shadows, or occluders;
- use the same sampled material identity in both views and throughout an
  episode, with view-specific illumination;
- composite only the visible mask so the gripper remains in front;
- avoid frame-independent noise or colors, which create temporal flicker;
- include some unchanged frames/episodes so the original-box distribution is
  not forgotten;
- inspect boundary halos and video compression after re-encoding.

Color jitter over the whole image is a useful nuisance augmentation but is not
equivalent to object-appearance augmentation.  It changes camera/background
statistics and does not break the carton-specific texture shortcut.

## Derived dataset and LeRobot compatibility

Keep the source tree immutable.  Create separate roots for highlighted and
highlighted+augmented datasets.  Copy metadata/state/action tables and generate
new videos with exactly 25,062 frames per camera at 30 fps, preserving episode
timestamp boundaries, resolution, camera keys, and frame ordering.  Give each
derived dataset a new `repo_id` and record source hashes, annotation version,
renderer version, and random seed.  Re-run the same audit before training.

LeRobot 0.6.1 loads the current dataset successfully with `video_backend=pyav`
and returns float RGB CHW tensors in [0,1].  ACT requires the exact input keys
and shapes saved in the checkpoint.  The saved visual normalizer uses ImageNet
mean `[0.485, 0.456, 0.406]` and std `[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]`; state/action stats
must remain identical because their underlying arrays are unchanged.

Fine-tune by warm-starting with the checkpoint's `pretrained_model` path into a
new output directory.  Do not use resume: the optimizer state is absent.  Before
an expensive run, execute a one-batch/one-step smoke test that confirms:

- both camera keys decode from the derived dataset;
- preprocessor output shapes and ranges match the saved ACT contract;
- state/action normalizer tensors exactly match the checkpoint;
- visual normalization remains ImageNet-based;
- a backward pass, optimizer step, save, reload, and finite inference all pass.

After a derived root passes validation, the LeRobot 0.6.1 command shape is:

```bash
lerobot-train \
  --dataset.repo_id=<derived_repo_id> \
  --dataset.root=<absolute_derived_dataset_root> \
  --dataset.video_backend=pyav \
  --policy.path=/home/manashvi/so101_outputs/variable_box_40episodes_act/checkpoints/050000/pretrained_model \
  --output_dir=<new_arm_specific_output_directory> \
  --steps=<small_matched_smoke_or_sweep_budget> \
  --batch_size=16 \
  --policy.device=cuda \
  --wandb.enable=false
```

Do not add `--resume=true`.  Use a different output directory for every arm and
seed, and freeze the final values only after the one-step compatibility check.

Use a low learning rate, save early checkpoints, and select step count from a
small validation sweep rather than assuming another 50k steps is beneficial.
Freeze neither backbone nor transformer by default until the smoke experiment
compares full fine-tuning with a short backbone-freeze warm-up.  All arms must
use the same seed set, batches, optimizer settings, gradient-step budget, and
original/derived sampling ratio.

## Controlled experiment and evaluation

Required arms:

- **A0:** untouched original 50k ACT checkpoint.
- **A1:** matched-step warm-start on original frames.  This controls for extra
  optimization and should accompany the user's A baseline.
- **B:** matched-step warm-start on highlighted original frames.
- **C:** matched-step warm-start on highlighted plus appearance-augmented
  frames, mixed with unchanged/highlight-only frames.

If resources allow, add augmentation-only as the missing cell of a 2x2
highlight x augmentation design.  It is the cleanest way to attribute gains.

Lock robot setup, cameras, lighting ranges, start-pose bins, target location,
policy horizon, safety limits, and success definition before trials.  Randomize
model trial order.  Use multiple repeated trials per object and start-pose bin;
report success with confidence intervals plus grasp, transport, placement, and
mask-acquisition failures separately.

Evaluate three non-overlapping stages:

1. **Original-box retention:** the training carton, with held-out start poses and
   lighting.  This detects catastrophic forgetting.
2. **Zero-shot unseen objects:** pre-registered colored boxes that match the
   graspable geometry first, followed by a separate geometry-shift set.  No
   demonstrations or tuning from these trials may enter this stage.  Report
   oracle-mask policy and online-mask system results separately.
3. **Additional-real-data learning:** only after stage 2 is frozen, collect a
   fixed demonstration budget (for example 5, then 10 per unseen-object group)
   and train matched variants.  Plot success versus real-demo count.  Never pool
   these results with zero-shot generalization.

Geometry-matched colored boxes test appearance generalization.  Boxes with
different dimensions, stiffness, lid topology, mass, or friction test a harder
appearance-plus-dynamics shift and must be reported separately.  Annotation
alone should not be expected to resolve changed grasp geometry or dynamics.

## Prototype limitations

The current two polygons are coarse manual visible-object annotations on one
frame from episode 15.  They demonstrate rendering only and are explicitly not
training labels.  The augmentation does not yet model view-dependent specular
effects, face-consistent texture coordinates, or video temporal coherence.
No full derived dataset has been produced and no training has begun.
